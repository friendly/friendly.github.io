## Battle of the Agents: My Claude Against the World

Trigger: an unsolicited "AI-gen-smelling" outreach email offering to help publicize
Vis-MLM-book (21 stars, "not enough people getting past the first five seconds," free
README audit, no pitch attached -- classic cold-outreach template). Sender: a
disposable-looking personal Gmail (`bodassss3456@gmail.com`), not a real company domain.

### Opening line (keep this)

"Out of the blue today, I got an email offering help with my Vis-MLM book ..."

### The swerve

Don't let the title over-promise a real fight. There wasn't one -- I never replied.
The honest arc is:

1. Set up the "battle" premise with some relish: sic my agent on their agent, ask
   Claude to draft a takedown. It obliges, and it's a genuinely sharp reply.
2. Swerve early (don't bury it at the end): the actual advice from my own agent was
   *don't send it*. Replying to spam -- even a witty dismissal -- just proves to the
   sender (or whatever list/service is behind them) that the mailbox is live and
   monitored, which gets you more outreach, not less.
3. Land on the more interesting, slightly subversive point: the smart move against
   an automated pitch is non-engagement, full stop. The "win" is a reply that exists
   but never gets sent.

Keep the fun flavor even after the swerve -- this is a light field-note, not a lecture
on email hygiene. The anticlimax is the joke, not a scold.

### Threads to pull

- Tell-tale signs of templated AI outreach: "quick question," "I ask because,"
  the manufactured personalization (my repo's star count as the whole "insight"),
  "I'm not selling anything" as the setup for an eventual sell.
- The burner-Gmail pattern as its own small tell -- worth a line on how to eyeball
  an account name (`bodassss3456`) as evidence of bulk/automated sending rather than
  a real consultant.
- The meta bit: asking my own assistant to evaluate whether a piece of writing is
  itself AI-generated, and to help me respond -- a small, funny instance of agents
  brokering a human interaction that never needed to happen.
- Maybe include (lightly edited) the actual reply Claude drafted, as an artifact of
  the exercise, with a caption like "the reply that never got sent."
- Practical takeaway readers can actually use: reporting as spam/junk vs. replying
  -- what each one costs and buys you. (Personal note: reporting as junk in Outlook
  made the email vanish immediately, which was oddly satisfying and worth a line.)

### Tone notes

- Self-aware about the "my agent vs. your agent" framing being a bit silly -- lean
  into that rather than taking it too seriously.
- Don't be preachy about AI slop or scold the sender; observational and amused beats
  indignant.
- Short piece. This is a field note, not an essay.

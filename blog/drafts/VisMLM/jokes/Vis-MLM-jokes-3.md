# More Vis-MLM jokes: human dramas in many dimensions

I read all three joke files and browsed the book’s relevant chapters. My favorites among the existing ones are the covariance matrix putting you fifth, the multivariate detective noir, and Roy at the bar. They have a situation and a payoff; some of the others explain a statistical idea and then attach a joke.

I’d push further toward little human dramas where the statistical property *causes* the punchline. Here are my strongest candidates.

## 1. Collinearity — the reference letter

“I can recommend either predictor without reservation. Unfortunately, I’ve never seen them work independently.”

## 2. Masking — the disciplinary hearing

Two outliers were called before the committee.  
“We checked them individually,” said the chair. “Each has an alibi supplied by the other.”

This draws on the chapter’s discussion of cases concealing one another’s influence. [Chapter 14](https://friendly.github.io/Vis-MLM-book/14-infl-robust.html)

## 3. Simpson’s paradox — couples counseling

“Things improve whenever we’re apart.”  
“And when you’re together?”  
“The trend reverses.”

## 4. Mahalanobis distance — the tailor

“The sleeves are normal. The trousers are normal. The chest is normal.”  
“Then why doesn’t the suit fit?”  
“We’ve never had all three on the same customer.”

## 5. Ridge regression — academic maturity

Early in his career, the coefficient swung wildly between large positive and large negative opinions. After regularization, he said less and could usually be found in the same place.

## 6. The bivariate ridge trace — the intervention

“You’ve moved away from the truth.”  
“Yes, but my colleagues no longer need a search party to locate me.”

These two use the book’s particular emphasis on showing the gain in precision alongside shrinkage. [Chapter 9](https://friendly.github.io/Vis-MLM-book/09-collinearity-ridge.html)

## 7. Small principal components — the audit

The first principal component explained most of the organization.  
The last one found where the money went.

## 8. Equality of covariance matrices — the marriage

“We agree on everything on average.”  
“Then what’s the problem?”  
“We fall apart in completely different directions.”

Both exploit the book’s attention to small dimensions and to differences in covariance structure beyond differences in means. [Chapter 13](https://friendly.github.io/Vis-MLM-book/13-eqcov.html)

## 9. HE plots — the building permit

The Hypothesis ellipse applied for an extension.  
Error objected.  
Roy inspected the boundary and said, “You’ve already built it.”

This uses the protrusion rule under Roy-based significance scaling. [Chapter 12](https://friendly.github.io/Vis-MLM-book/12-mlm-viz.html)

## 10. The penguins — the identity check

The discriminant analyst asked the penguin its bill length, bill depth, flipper length and body mass.  
“Why don’t you just ask my species?”  
“That would be cheating.”

## 11. PCA versus discriminant analysis — the seating plan

PCA arranged the dinner guests to show off their differences.  
Discriminant analysis asked who was related, then put the families as far apart as possible.

These follow the distinction between displaying overall variation and finding directions that separate known groups. [Chapter 15](https://friendly.github.io/Vis-MLM-book/21-discrim.html)

## 12. The romance — conditional affection

“You used to say we had a strong relationship.”  
“We did. Then I met your covariates.”

## Cartoon candidates

My strongest cartoon candidates are **the reference letter**, **the tailor**, and **the covariance marriage**. The tailor is especially promising: three individually ordinary measurements, one visibly impossible suit, and a deadpan explanation. It makes the multivariate idea visible before the reader finishes the caption.

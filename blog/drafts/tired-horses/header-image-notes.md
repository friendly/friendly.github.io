# Header image notes: *All the Tired Horses*

Created with Codex image generation on 2026-08-07.

## Michael's initial direction

> Someone like me, sitting at a desk with a computer, 2 monitors, perhaps in a rustic cabin. But a herd of wild horses starts flying or jumping in.

The image is intended for the header or near the top of the draft post. Michael also asked that the prompt and conversation about the image be preserved.

## Final generation prompt

```text
Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: wide header or near-top image for a thoughtful personal blog post about the technical writing process
Primary request: Create a cinematic editorial illustration of an older male academic writer seated at a wooden desk in a rustic cabin, working at a computer with two monitors, while a herd of wild horses suddenly sweeps into and through the room—some galloping, some leaping with almost dreamlike weightlessness. The horses embody distracting but generative technical tasks bursting into the act of writing.
Scene/backdrop: Warm, lived-in timber cabin study with books, papers, a mug, and natural outdoor light; the boundary between cabin and open landscape becomes surreal where the horses enter.
Subject: Thoughtful older writer seen in three-quarter view or partly from behind, focused yet startled, hands near keyboard; two clearly visible computer monitors showing subtle, non-legible hints of writing, code, and a chart.
Style/medium: Sophisticated painterly editorial illustration, grounded and human, gently humorous, magical realism rather than fantasy spectacle; rich natural texture, suitable for an intelligent academic blog.
Composition/framing: Wide landscape banner, approximately 16:9. Writer and desk in the left or center-left foreground, horses flowing diagonally through the right and background, strong sense of motion without hiding the writer or both monitors. Leave calm negative space near the upper-left/upper-center for an overlaid blog title. Must crop well as a wide header.
Lighting/mood: Warm amber cabin light mixed with cool daylight, energetic and slightly chaotic but inviting, not frightening.
Color palette: Weathered wood, warm ochre, muted blue-gray, chestnut, black, cream, dusty sunlight.
Constraints: No words, captions, logos, watermarks, readable interface text, extra limbs, distorted monitors, or injured animals. Exactly one writer, exactly two monitors. Horses should look anatomically convincing even when leaping. No cowboy imagery.
```

## Result and possible revision directions

The first result is a wide painterly scene with the writer at left and a large herd entering from the open right side of the cabin. It is warm, energetic, and more spectacular than comic. The generated image contains no title text, leaving typography to the webpage.

Possible later variants: make the room more enclosed so the horses seem to burst physically through the workspace; reduce the herd for a quieter image; make the situation funnier and less epic; or use a reference photograph if a closer likeness is wanted.

# Brain full of tasks illustration

## Michael's initial direction

> Get ChatGPT to generate an image of a brain containing many small boxes, each representing a different task — some demanding attention, some pushed to the back burner, but all still present.

Follow-up direction: not painterly; more graphic brain detail than a pure conceptual diagram, with the brain forming the background. Use a mixture of shapes representing concrete tasks.

## Final generation prompt

```text
Use case: infographic-diagram
Asset type: near-top conceptual illustration for a personal academic blog post about cognitive overload during technical writing
Primary request: Create a polished conceptual image of a human brain containing many small compartments, boxes, circles, tabs, and irregular panels, each representing a concrete task that remains mentally present. Some tasks urgently demand attention, some are actively being worked on, and others have visibly receded to the back burner without disappearing.
Scene/backdrop: A large, detailed side-view brain fills most of a wide landscape composition and forms the subdued background framework. Show recognizable folds and neural texture with refined graphic detail, but keep it clearly illustrative rather than medical or photorealistic.
Internal task imagery: Within the brain, arrange many distinct task compartments containing clear miniature visual symbols and tiny scenes: a document with a blinking cursor for writing; code brackets and a bug for software debugging; a small chart or scatterplot; stacked reference papers and citation cards; an image-search/contact-sheet panel; an email envelope with an alert; Git branches and commit nodes; a calendar/clock; a package/toolbox; two computer windows; a lightbulb or scribbled note for a new idea; and several partially obscured unfinished tasks. Use icons and visual metaphors, not readable words.
Hierarchy and state: A few foreground compartments should glow, pulse, protrude, or overlap to show urgent demands. Several mid-level tasks should be crisp and active. Back-burner tasks should sit deeper in shadow, muted but still plainly visible. Thin connecting lines and looping arrows may show attention jumping between tasks, without turning the image into a rigid flowchart.
Style/medium: Sophisticated contemporary editorial graphic with dimensional layering, crisp linework, subtle collage and cutaway qualities. More visually detailed than a pure conceptual diagram, but not painterly. Intelligent, slightly playful, and suitable for an academic blog.
Composition/framing: Wide 16:9 landscape image. Brain centered and fully visible with modest negative space around it. Strong visual hierarchy at web size. No title or caption inside the image.
Lighting/mood: Cool neutral background with warm amber/red accents for urgent tasks, brighter cream/cyan for active tasks, and desaturated blue-gray for deferred tasks. Busy and mentally crowded, but legible rather than chaotic.
Constraints: No readable text, no logos, no watermark, no grotesque biological imagery, no exposed head or person, no literal flames, no generic corporate stock-art look. Avoid making every compartment identical. Ensure the brain anatomy reads clearly behind the task mosaic.
```

## Result

The first generation uses a detailed side-view brain as a dark structural frame. Concrete task panels show writing, debugging, charts, image search, references, email, version control, scheduling, tools, and unfinished work. Warm red/amber panels demand attention, cyan panels read as active, and dim blue-gray panels remain visible in the background.

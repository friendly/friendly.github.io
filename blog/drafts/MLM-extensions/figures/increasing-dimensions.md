# Increasing dimensions banner

Created for “On Extending lm() Methods to mlm()” with Codex’s built-in image generator.

## Design discussion

The initial brief was to make an artistic blog banner from scratch, with vectors-matrices-tensors.webp in the Dropbox Documents/images/MatrixAlgebra folder as a conceptual reference. The design should look like a print made by a human designer. The draft’s “one more dimension” theme and the blue and orange of its dimension-ladder figure suggested a letterpress composition. We also considered the encounter between A. Square and the Sphere in *Flatland*.

The first preview showed a vector, a matrix, and a stack of matrices. The revision request was:

> Include a scalar as the 0-dim case. at the left. Maybe also add a large curved thick green arrow from bottom left to upper right, symbolizing an increase in dimensionality. The arrow could also grow somewhat thicker from left to right. Add just of bit of texture to the background, perhaps representing old paper, and frayed edges. But be prepared to back out of any of these later details so they don't get to be too much

The revised preview was approved as increasing-dimensions.png. To revisit the balance of the design, the scalar should stay. The arrow is the first element to soften if it draws too much attention.

## Initial generation prompt

> Use case stylized-concept. Generate a wide landscape blog banner for a statistician’s essay on one more dimension, from lm to mlm. Original understated mid-century mathematical book-cover illustration with the tactile finish of a two-ink letterpress print on warm ivory stock. Composition is one visual thought, horizontal, generous margins, all artwork comfortably inside the central 65 percent height for potential wide crop. On left a single upright indigo strip of 5 contiguous square cells, precisely drawn. In center this same five-cell indigo strip becomes the leftmost column of a rectangular, planar grid as two burnt-orange columns join it, all cells matching size. At right, a much larger rendering of the same blue-and-orange grid with two identical rectangular grid sheets visibly separated behind it, all parallel, offset in a consistent isometric direction, evoking three slices of a 3-way array. Preserve the clear transition from column to plane to stack, no intermediate objects. Fine ink rules, subtle paper grain, tasteful quiet color, strong legibility at thumbnail size. Should look like an actual restrained graphic print from a skilled human book designer. No type, no letters, no numerals, no captions, no arrows, no other motifs, no gradients or glossy 3D effects.

## Revision prompt

The generator used the first preview as its image reference.

> Revise the previous 2:1 wide letterpress banner, preserving restrained vintage book-cover aesthetic and navy plus burnt orange ink. Exactly four clearly spaced objects aligned along one baseline in middle-lower band: (1) single navy square scalar at far left, matching cell size; (2) navy column of five square cells; (3) five-row three-column matrix, navy first column and ochre-orange next two; (4) precisely three identical parallel slices of that matrix visibly offset up-right. Recompose slightly smaller to fit them all. Add ONE long gracefully curving deep muted forest-green directional arrow in clear space: starts at lower left below scalar, sweeps right along beneath all four objects, curls upwards in clear margin to the right of the stack and ends at upper right; keep the trajectory entirely outside the artwork and maintain breathing room. Tail relatively fine and shaft grows gradually thicker, broad arrowhead, handcrafted clean printed contour. Very light warm ivory aged paper, much subtler and finer texture than reference, barely perceptible fraying at perimeter, no grunge. Preserve ample safe margins and clear visual hierarchy. No text, numerals, border or additional decoration.
